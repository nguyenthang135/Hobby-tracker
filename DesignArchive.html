05 · ITERATION LOG

Before → After

These are explanatory visual reconstructions derived from our discussion. They are NOT original version screenshots.

Historical provenance: we preserved the decision trail from the conversation, but exact visual captures of every rejected draft are not available here. The panels below are labeled concept reconstructions; do not use them as falsely dated screenshots.
01

Home scheduling

Revised
BEFORE · CONCEPT RECREATIONEarlier Home

An activity list without a visible way to add a plan

AFTER · APPROVED DIRECTIONHome V4

A primary “+ Schedule” action beside daily activities

Why: An obvious next step is necessary for the scheduling flow.

02

My Hobbies ownership

Revised
BEFORE · CONCEPT RECREATIONCatalog-first V1

Explore a list of predefined Photography / Guitar / Badminton hobbies

AFTER · APPROVED DIRECTIONCustom V2

Create any hobby from scratch; pick name, optional description and priority

Why: Users own the definition of their interests; discovery is not forced.

03

Creator personalization

Revised
BEFORE · CONCEPT RECREATIONIntegrated V5 regression

A text dropdown saying “Spark” instead of visual icons

AFTER · APPROVED DIRECTIONCreator V3

A 12-icon grid, six color swatches, live preview and priority choice cards

Why: Recognition is faster than remembering icon names.

04

Authentication navigation

Revised
BEFORE · CONCEPT RECREATIONTemporary post-login screen

A Sign Out button directly under the welcome flow

AFTER · APPROVED DIRECTIONApproved navigation

Sign In → Home; Sign Out only in Settings with confirmation

Why: Account controls belong in conventional settings navigation.

05

Theme behavior

Revised
BEFORE · CONCEPT RECREATIONLight-only integration

Appearance showed seven cards but six were labeled “After approval”

AFTER · APPROVED DIRECTIONSeven selectable themes

Theme-specific shapes and palette persist across views; data is retained

Why: Appearance now changes the actual UI instead of displaying inert samples.

09 · TEACHER'S RUBRIC

UI Simplicity / Visual Density

Five categories × four criteria × five points = 100. This archive displays the rubric; the Specification contains the scoring form.

20 PTS

A · Text & Content

  1. Short, scannable headings and labels
  2. Remove unnecessary introductory text
  3. Hide infrequently used information in details
  4. Avoid repeated information across cards
20 PTS

B · Color Discipline

  1. One primary accent and consistent neutral palette
  2. No arbitrary card decoration colors
  3. Consistent success / warning / error meanings
  4. Never communicate status by color alone
20 PTS

C · Density & Whitespace

  1. Density appropriate for task and device
  2. Consistent spacing scale
  3. Group related content; avoid excessive cards
  4. Readable, usable tables, forms and controls
20 PTS

D · Visual Hierarchy

  1. Important content immediately recognizable
  2. Primary actions dominate secondary actions
  3. Distinct heading / body / metadata hierarchy
  4. Sections do not compete for attention
20 PTS

E · Consistency & Accessibility

  1. Consistent components, type, spacing and iconography
  2. Body text contrast at least 4.5:1, subject to WCAG exceptions
  3. Visible interaction states
  4. Clear loading, empty, success, error states
No invented score. This is a reference-only audit display, not proof of 85/100. The teacher also recommends a 5-second test, grayscale test and reduction test. WCAG accessibility requires separate verification.
